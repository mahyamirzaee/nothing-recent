Nothing Recent

Speculative HCI · Research through Design · 2026

A warning app for women under compulsory hijab that admits, on every screen, that it cannot keep anyone safe.

In Iran, compulsory hijab is enforced in the street by patrols. Apps that share their whereabouts are not hypothetical: Gershad crowdsourced them on a map in 2016. This prototype takes that familiar form — reported sightings, nearby, minutes old — and designs against its central promise. It opens as a knitting row counter, because the phone is the thing that gets searched.

Research question

How might an interface designed for protection acknowledge, in its own form, that it cannot protect the person using it?

The interaction

  1. 01 · Cover. The app opens as ROWS, a stitch counter that genuinely counts rows. This is what anyone holding the phone sees. One tap, locking the screen or switching apps returns to it.
  2. 02 · Open. A long press of 1.2 seconds on the row number fades the counter away: sightings nearby, how long ago, how many people said so.
  3. 03 · Fade. Every sighting dies after forty minutes, fading the whole way. When they are gone the screen says so, and says that this is not the same as the street being clear.

A working browser prototype. Every sighting in it is invented: there is no location, no account and no server, nothing is stored and nothing is sent. It senses nothing about the street, and it protects no one.

Situated context

What if a safety app said plainly that it cannot keep you safe?

Avoidance as a daily routine

Compulsory hijab is enforced not only in law but in ordinary places: a metro entrance, a square, the gate of a park. Avoiding enforcement becomes routine — looking ahead, guessing, taking another street. The prototype places itself inside that routine, and inside the phone, which is also the object that can be taken and read.

Tensions

Not problems the design solves. Tensions it holds open, and keeps visible.

  • WarningEvidence The message that warns you is the proof that you knew.
  • CareRecord To look after someone, something has to be written down.
  • SilenceSafety An empty screen reads as calm. It only means nobody spoke.
  • CoverSuspicion Hiding the thing makes the hiding itself worth searching for.
  • MineOurs A sighting is only useful once shared, and sharing draws the network.

Reflection

Nothing Recent is an exploratory design artefact, not a validated intervention and not a safety tool. There is no study behind it and no claim that it helps. It does not speak for women living in Iran; it is a design question asked from my own position, as an Iranian designer working in Germany.

Future direction · Carrying nothing

A further iteration would move away from the device altogether, and ask what a warning could be if it left no record: something held in cloth, in a gesture, in an arrangement between people, that cannot be opened by a stranger holding your phone. The digital version would then become the thing it is already pretending not to be — a trace.

Design intentionCould care travel between women without leaving a record that can be used against them?
